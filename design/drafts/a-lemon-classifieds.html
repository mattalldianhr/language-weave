Reading, as usual

Most of what you read in a day is not a book. It is a tab.

A recipe, a thread, a long article you will probably not finish, something about la ciudad you might visit next spring. You read it quickly, and then you read the next one. Nothing much happens to you.

But it adds up: a few thousand words before lunch, a few thousand more after, cada día, without really meaning to. If a few of those words came back un poco different, you would hardly notice. Quizás that is the point.

How it works

Three beats, and that is the whole trick.

1

Rare, and only where it is obvious.

One word in fifty, at first. Nunca more than one in twenty. Each swap lands where the sentence already tells you what it means, so you keep reading at full speed.

2

Hover for English. Click to flip.

Hover la palabra and the English appears. Click, and it flips back and stays flipped. Nouns bring their article along.

It comes back until it sticks.

A word you met in la mañana returns on later days, at wider and wider gaps, about ten meetings in all. Then it is yours, and a nuevo one takes its place.

la casa
das Haus

Nouns arrive with their article, so gender comes for free.

Classified

The evidence, filed under the usual headings. Numbers as measured; the workings are in the research folder.

Wanted

Wanted — A useful word list. The 100 most common English words cover 46% of web text, pero they are almost all grammar words you can’t swap. A useful list starts around 300 words and is mostly nouns and connectors. Serious inquiries only.

Lost & found

Lost — One word, met fewer than 8 times. Nada was recalled three months later. A word needs about 8–12 meetings before it stays. If found, please return on a later day.

Found — One word, met a menudo: about twelve meetings, spaced over a few weeks. It stayed. Owner may keep it.

For sale

For sale — Naive word-for-word swapping. Right only 63% of the time. Muy cheap. As is.

Also available — Part-of-speech checks and a sense whitelist. Juntos, they lift that to 82%. The remaining errors are idioms and senses; a verifier catches those.

Notices

Notice — Keep foreign words siempre under 1 in 20 of the text, nearer 1 in 50, so comprehension stays above 95%. — The Management

Personals

Noun seeks article — Learning a noun without its article makes learning its gender harder later. Seeking un amigo for life. Must agree in gender and number.
