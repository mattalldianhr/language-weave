## Design Context

### Users
People who read a lot on the web in English and want to pick up a second language without studying. Many install tools through a coding agent (Claude Code, Codex), so they are comfortable with a repo and a one-line setup prompt. They meet Language Weave on its homepage while deciding whether to install it, then live with it inside pages they were already going to read.

### Brand Personality
Playful and a little weird. Words sneak into your reading and you catch them in the act. Mischief and surprise, never gamification. Three words: sneaky, bookish, delighted. The product never makes the language the topic; the homepage shouldn't either — it demonstrates by doing.

### Aesthetic Direction
Visual direction is chosen per build from random seeds (art-directing-ui), so no house style is fixed yet. Hard anti-references: generic SaaS landing (gradient hero, three feature cards, testimonial strip, pricing table); Duolingo-style gamification (mascots, streaks, confetti, candy palette); dark hacker glow (black, neon cyan, terminal cosplay); academic paper (dense grey figure-heavy pages). The page is demo-led: its own copy carries woven foreign words with a hover gloss.

### Design Principles
1. Show, don't pitch: the first thing a visitor does is hover a word and understand it.
2. Evidence is part of the charm: the research numbers appear as asides and marginalia, not as a stats block.
3. Reading comfort over spectacle: the page is itself a reading experience; measure 65–75ch, generous leading.
4. One strong idea per section, no filler sections; no cards-in-cards, no icon-above-heading grids.
5. Honest about the method: swaps are few (1 in 50 to 1 in 20 words), spaced, glossed in English.
